## Why does a resume matter?

Your resume is the first thing many recruiters, employers, and hiring managers will look at first. It's important to make a strong statement with what they first see, as well as creating a resume that uniquely and fairly demonstrates your abilities!

Secondly, resumes specifically for Computer Science and tech in general are __A LOT__ different from many other fields. So often, it can be difficult to find a good resource in building a good resume that is catered for the tech industry.

Alright, so let's get started!

## What should be included in a resume?

There's many different parts I would say are necessary towards creating a "good" resume in Computer Science. I want you to note that this is not exhaustive, but definitely a good starting point.

1. __Contact Information__
    - Name
    - Phone Number
    - Email
These are all critical information that a hiring manager will NEED in order to contact you. An important note is that physical address really isn't necessary anymore, so I would say you shouldn't include it.

2. __Socials__
    - LinkedIn
    - GitHub
    - Personal Website
    These all are other resources that flaunt what you're capable of. Use them so they can see more about who you are since resumes are limited in space. 

3. __Education__ 
    Alright so we're all college students here, this is kind of a gimme since you're applying for new grad positions. You want to include some crucial information about your education though, such as:

    - __School Name__
    - __Degree__ (Bachelors of Science, Computer Science or even just BSc in Computer Science)
    - Actual/Expected Graduationc Date (Expected May 2025 for example)
    - __GPA__ (personally I would say only if it's above a 3.0)
    - __Relevant Courses__(cut this out if you need space)
    - __Awards/Honors__(if applicable)

4. __Work Experience__
    Depending on your experience, I would say to put this above your education. For example, if you've had some internships or co-ops, definitely put this section above your education. Also, only put work experience this is relevant to tech/computer science. No need to share with a tech company that you worked at a grocery store for 3 years (although put this on LinedIn if you have no other experience).

    - __Company Name__
    - __Position Title__
    - __Dates of Employment__
    - __Location__ (City, State)
    - __Description of what you did__ (2-3 bullet points that get straight to the point. First 1-2 bullets focus on what you did and what was actually accomplished, last bullet point about the relevant technologies you used).

5. __Projects__
    This is a great way to show off what you're capable of. I would say to include 2-3 projects that you're most proud of. This can be a school project, personal project, or even a hackathon project. Also, __ALWAYS ALWAYS ALWAYS__ tailor the projects your including to the position you're applying for!! Don't give them a Python project if you're applying for a Java position.

    - __Project Name__
    - __Description__ (1-2 sentences)
    - __Technologies Used__ (1-2 bullet points)
    - __Link to Repository/Website__ (Use GitFront for private repos)

6. __Skills__
    This is where you want to list practically everything you know something about. If you've used it before, no matter how minimally, put it here. Experience is experience. I'd suggest ordering it strongest at the beginning and weakest at the end, and saying that next to the section!

    - __Languages__ (Java, Python, C++, etc.)
    - __Frameworks__ (Flutter, React, Django, etc.)
    - __Tools__ (Git, Docker, Linux, etc.)

7. __Certifications__
    If you have any certifications, I would say to include them here. This can be a Udemy course, a Coursera course, or even a certification from a hackathon.

8. __Achievements or Activities__
    This is a section I like to include so the hiring manager can see what you're involved in. This can be a club, a sport, or even a volunteer organization. It's a great way to show that you're more than just a student, and can show your personality a little more. If possible though, you can tailor them to the field (such as member of the Computer Science Club!).

Lastly, you're resume is __never__ going to be perfect. You'll make a correction every time you look at it. And that's okay! Just make the best resume you can and keep improving it as you go.

## What should I avoid in a resume?

0. __More than one page__
    This is a mandatory rule in my opinion. I've known hiring managers who literally throw out resumes that are more than one page. It's not worth the risk.

1. __Irrelevant Information__
    This is a big one. If it's not relevant to the position you're applying for, don't include it. It's just taking up space that could be used for something that is relevant.

2. __Too Much Information__
    This is a resume, not a novel. Keep it short and sweet. Provides highlights that leave room for a conversation during an interview.

3. __Lack of Keywords__
    This is a big one. Many companies use an ATS (Applicant Tracking System) to filter out resumes that don't have the right keywords. Make sure you're using the right keywords for the position you're applying for. Luckily, a lot of these are covered in the technical skills section so make sure you're including those!

4. __Lack of Proofreading__
    This is a big one. Make sure you're proofreading your resume. Have a friend, family member, or even a professor look over it. It's important to have a second pair of eyes on it.

## Resource for building a strong resume 

1. __Jake's Resume__
    Some people shy away from using a template, but they work man. If you don't know where to start at all, this is a resource I used to start my own resume and still use often. Here's a link to [Jake's Resume](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/jakes-resume/syzfjbzwjncs).

2. __GitFront__
    Use this to show off projects locked behind private repositories. This is key to showing what you're capable of programming, especially if you don't have any side projects. Here's a link to [GitFront.io](https://gitfront.io/).

3. __Other Students__
    This is a big one. If you're not sure about something, ask a friend or upper-classman who you think has a good resume or has been successful in CS. I personally got most of my help from my friends and it's helped me a lot. I'll plug myself as a resource as well, please let me know if you'd want me to review your resume!

4. __Career Services__
    WCU has a fair amount of resources that can help you build a resume. I would suggest going to them if you've completed your resume and just want someone else to look over it. They may be able to provide some feedback.

5. __Alumni, Professors, or Professionals__
    If you know someone who is in the field, ask them to look over your resume. They can provide some great feedback and may even be able to help you get your foot in the door at a company.
