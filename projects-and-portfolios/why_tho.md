## Why Projects?

When hoping to work in software development, or any other tech related field, a degree is a good thing to have. A formal education offers you fundamental understandings of computer science
principles, gives you experience working in teams, and gets you access to professors who can help you learn. However, a degree is often not enough, especially in the tech industry. Employers
want to see that you can apply what you've learned in a real-world setting. Additionally, degree programs are often constrained in what they're able to teach, be it because of time or
personel. This is where projects come in.

Projects are great ways to learn skills that you might not have been able to learn in a classroom setting. They allow you to learn new technologies that employers might be looking for, and
they give you a chance to use your skills to solve real problems. Projects are a great way to flex your creativity and show off your technical skills. They can also be a great way to show
employers that you're passionate about what you do.

## Okay, but why a portfolio?

A portfolio is a collection of your work that you can show to potential employers. It's a central location to show off the projects that you're most proud of. A portfolio can be a great way
to show employers that you have the skills they're looking for, by showcasing the work you've done in the past. Portfolios themselves can be projects. They're a great way to show off your
design skills and a great way to show off your personality and your interests.
