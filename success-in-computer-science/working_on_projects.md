# 2 - Working on Projects

[Home](README.md)

In this section, we will talk about working on projects in the CS program. Projects are a big part of the CS program, and they typically make up a large portion of your grade in most classes. Most projects are also assigned in groups of 2-4 students. Here are some tips for working on projects:

## 2.1 Communication

Communication is key when working on a project with a group. When you're assigned a new project with someone you haven't met yet, the easiest way to get in contact with them is by quickly meeting after class or over email. After that, you can exchange phone numbers or other contact information, such as Discord, to make communication easier.

When working on a project, it's important to keep in touch with your group members. The first thing you might do is assign everyone different parts of the project to work on. This is fine, but it's important to check in with each other regularly to make sure everyone is on the same page. Also be sure to ask your partners for help if you ever get stuck on something; two heads are better than one!

## 2.2 Version Control

If you haven't heard of it yet, **version control** is a system that allows you to keep track of changes to your code. This is especially useful when working on a project with a group, as it allows you to see who made what changes and when. The most popular version control system is Git, which is used in the industry and is a great skill to have. **GitHub** is a website that hosts Git repositories, and it's a great place to store your code and collaborate with others.

Learning to use Git and GitHub will significantly improve your workflow when working on projects. It will also help you learn how to work with others on a project, which is a valuable skill to have in the industry. There are many online resources to learn Git, such as the Codecademy [Learn Git and GitHub](https://www.codecademy.com/learn/learn-git) course, or our very own [Git and GitHub Workshop](../github/README.md)!

## 2.3 Time Management

CS projects typically have a timeline of two weeks. At first, this may seem like a lot of time, but it can go by quickly. It's important to manage your time wisely when working on a project. Make sure to start early and work on it a little bit each day. This will help you avoid the stress of trying to finish everything at the last minute. We'll talk more about time management in the next section.

## 2.4 Asking for Help

If you ever get stuck on a project, don't be afraid to ask for help! Your professors and classmates are there to help you, and they want to see you succeed. Students are not allowed to share code with each other, but you can ask for help with debugging or understanding a concept. You can also visit your professor during office hours.

Additionally, the Math and Computer Science Tutoring Center is a great resource for getting help with your projects. The tutors there are upperclassmen who have taken the classes you're in, and they can help you with your projects and answer any questions you have. They can't give you the answers, but they can help guide you in the right direction.

Lastly, one of the best tools in a programmer's toolbox is the ability to research. If you're stuck on something, try Googling it! There are many online resources that can help you understand a concept or solve a problem. Some popular resources include [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com/), [GeeksforGeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/), and [W3Schools](https://www.w3schools.com/).
