# 1 - Study Tips

[Home](README.md)

This part of the seminar will cover study tips that have been proven to be effective for computer
science students. These tips are based on both the personal experience of senior students and
research. Some of many of these points you may have already heard, but they are what proves to be
successful.

## 1.1 Background Noise and Music

Studies show that listening to music with lyrics in them can negatively impact information retention.
So, avoid busy-sounding environments to ensure material retention is at its maximum. Also, being in
a loud or busy environment can lead to distractions, so it is best avoid them when possible.

My personal recommendation is finding a study room in the library, dorm, or apartment. If that is
not possible, try to find a room with as few distractions as possible. For an extra layer against
distractions, I listen to piano music to prevent outside noise while also allowing my brain to
focus on what I'm studying.

## 1.2 Phone

A telephone is a massive distraction, and it can lead to very little work getting done over a long
period of time. A method I have started doing is I will work a set amount of time (30-45 mins), and
afterward I will look at my phone for a few minutes (like 5 minutes for example) before going back
to studying for an allotted time. This set schedule allows time to be tracked more effectively while
simultaneously breaking up the study session, making it feel less like a massive chunk of time that
you are sitting there doing something you are less interested in.

## 1.3 Note Card

You will find out very quickly as you move through the CS program here at WCU that writing note
cards will be a big study method. The professors allowing a note card for the tests is a very
effective strategy by the professors to try to get the students to study. If you ask most
upperclassmen, those who have written a notecard normally perform better on tests. Additionally,
they will tell you that they probably didn't have to use it that much when it came to test time.
The notecard is a great tool for studying the large amounts of information that is covered for tests.

#### 1.3.1: _ORGANIZE, ORGANIZE, ORGANIZE_

It is very important to keep the notecard neat and organized. Have a notecard with information all
over the face of the card will prove to be less effective for studying and even less effective for
reading during a test if required.

Spend the time to organize the note card into sections. I would often section off parts of the card
with lines and make a small label for each topic. That way, finding the topic you are looking for
is much easier.

#### 1.3.2: <u>_Do not write too small_</u>

If you write too small, you risk not being able to read the text come test time. However, writing
a sufficient amount is important to ensure you are adequately covering the test material. If you
use a pencil (recommended), use a 0.7 or 0.5 lead pencil to make sure your writing is as precise as
it can be.

## Tests

Tests are a huge part of the CS curriculum. Often times you'll be asked to memorize concepts, small details, and even code. Here are some tips for studying for tests:

### 1.4.1: _Start Early_

Starting early is crucial for studying for tests. The material you are studying is often complex and requires time to understand. Starting early allows you to ask questions, get help, and understand the material better.

### 1.4.2: _A little at a time_

Studying a little bit each day is much more effective than cramming the night before. Cramming can lead to stress, anxiety, and poor performance on the test. Studying a little bit each day allows you to understand the material better and retain it longer.

### 1.4.3: _Practice Problems_

Practice problems are a great way to prepare for tests. They help you understand the material better and give you a chance to apply what you've learned. Go through your previous tests, worksheets, homework and notes to find practice problems. If you're still struggling, ask your professor for more practice problems.
